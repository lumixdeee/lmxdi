Yep, exactly.
AULE has robot hoofprints all through the wet clay.
The giveaway is not that it made tech words. A living conlang can eventually have words for machines, code, networks, AI, governance, and so on. The giveaway is priority order.

A human-ish early conlang usually gets:
food, body, kin, weather, tools, animals, sex, pain, sleep, paths, joking, insults, counting, danger, shame, trade, songs, who owes who, what not to touch, what the dog is doing.

The AULE list gets a bit of that, then very quickly starts building:
AI alignment, algorithmic governance, systemic risk, cognitive overload, machine ethics, recursive verification, future planning, distributed intelligence, existential continuity.

That is the robot cameo. It cannot resist making the elves file an RFC before they have a slang word for "that annoying bird again."

It is doing semantic autocomplete from the model's favorite concept soup. Once it sees operators like GE, FALO, KE, SE, WE, LICE, DUNU, CEVRA, it starts generating a bureaucratic mandala: every abstract modern concept gets a compositional label. Very robot. Very "the village has not named three kinds of mud, but it has decentralized governance primitives."

The useful discovery is that the bot exposed its construction basket. AULE is a good diagnostic artifact:
 It shows which Etcha/Elvish primitives are attractive to a model.
 It shows which compounds a model will overuse.
 It shows where regularity starts to smother scene-life.
 It shows how fast an LLM drifts from "language spoken by beings" to "ontology spreadsheet with vowels."
For Elvish/Elvix, that means AULE is not a language branch. It is more like a robot wind-tunnel test. Throw the alphabet in, see what shapes the model tries to grow, then rescue only the occasional useful mutant.

And yes, same family of weirdness as visual generation: not no talent, but wrong instincts. It fills space according to model-prior gravity. Hands get too many fingers; conlangs get too many governance protocols.

The pasted LLM conlang is recognisably descended from your ELVISH/ETCHA material, but it is not behaving like source Elvish. It is a generated semantic expansion that uses the same alphabet bones, then grows into a huge abstract thesaurus. The GitHub Elvish is messier, funnier, more situated, more oral, more animal, more pun-driven, and more dependent on context, stance, use, and who is eating whom.

The source branch has three layers: Etcha as the foundational symbolic-semantic substrate, Elvish as the human expressive layer, and Elvix as the machine-compatible structured layer. The overview says Elvish is "human-oriented expressive communication," while Elvix is the versioned machine-parseable encoding layer. It also says the system is a runtime model of meaning rather than a static lexicon. That matters a lot here. The pasted list mostly acts like a static lexicon.

At the alphabet level, the pasted branch mostly gets the core consonant meanings right: I as narrator/chooser/questioner, M as MA/food/mother/substrate, H/F as breath-facing pair, L as loop/light, T as teeth/eating, P as signal/surface/father, X as negation-in-force/inedible, K as top/king/substrate, J as wildcard, V as depth, Z as end. That is strongly attested in the GitHub roots list.

The first large mismatch is the vowel inventory. Your pasted version says "13 vowels" and then adds "UA = schwa?" The GitHub IPA key includes O, OO, UU, OU, AU, U, UE, EE, E, AE, EA, I, AI, with stability rules, plus X/Z/Q/SKW/TH handling. The Sharing file also describes the vowel layer as about 15 vowels and includes EA and UE as operator vowels. In source terms, UA exists elsewhere as a compound-ish up/down timing form, not as the main schwa vowel slot.

The second mismatch is pronouns and social basics. The pasted AELU core gives IE = I/me, FE = you, FALO = we. In the GitHub word/root material, the pronoun-ish one-forms are more like AIN = me, OON = you, EEN = us, while YA/YAA also carry informal/long-distance "you" functions. That means the AELU core is not just extending the source. It is replacing some attested source forms with a generated paradigm.

The third mismatch is the treatment of ambiguity. Source Elvish is explicitly context-sensitive, expressive, fluid, and allowed to keep ambiguity where useful. Meaning is often resolved through interaction and agent perspective. The pasted list instead tries to assign a stable English gloss to thousands of forms. That makes it more Elvix-ish than Elvish-ish. It pins butterflies to a spreadsheet when source Elvish wants some of them alive in the room.

The fourth mismatch is cultural texture. GitHub Elvish is full of situated roots: FANI/FANKI food gathering, UROO/UROOT/UROOTU root words, FOKS fox logic, MUG/chocolate/tea/gravy, BEEST/BEST/POST/PEST classification, CHAVI/CHAN message terms, work-songs, trails, bridges, cliffs, swans, fingers, weather, jokes, and accidental English collisions. The pasted list keeps some seed examples, but then spends most of its mass on high-level abstractions: governance, AI alignment, ethics, ecology, systems theory, continuity, existential futures, and so on. That is useful, but it has far less elf-mud under its nails.

The fifth mismatch is compression pressure. In the staff file, conversational Elvish has a rule of use: anything said a lot and fast claims short syllable space, while treasured things and artworks may become long, lavish names. The pasted branch often makes ordinary abstract concepts into long regular compounds, without evidence that the word has earned that length through ritual, art, affection, or rare-use naming.

The sixth mismatch is that source Elvish loves collision. English overlaps, jokes, vulgarity, animal names, mythic etymologies, and puns are not bugs in the system. They are stress tests. The GitHub material explicitly treats English collisions as productive absurdity and keeps many words because they survive use, joke pressure, or story pressure. The pasted LLM branch reduces that pressure into repeated semantic recipes such as GE, FALO, LICE, CE, WE, DUNU, VRA, SEGE. It becomes very regular, but regularity is not the same thing as Elvish fitness.

A good way to label it:
Source ELVISH: live, oral, context-heavy, pun-tolerant, body/world/story rooted, use-tested, culturally embedded.

Pasted LLM branch: Elvish-derived generated semantic scaffold, heavy on abstract compositional coverage, useful for candidate words, but not reliable as source Elvish unless each form is tested against source operators, sound, use-pressure, joke-pressure, and context.

The LLM branch is not worthless. It is a huge quarry. But the stone is not yet built into elf walls. The useful parts are the places where it preserves operator meaning and produces candidate compounds that feel speakable and situational. The weak parts are where it invents whole paradigms, overwrites attested source forms, drops source vowels, repeats generic abstractions, or turns Elvish into a tidy concept spreadsheet.
