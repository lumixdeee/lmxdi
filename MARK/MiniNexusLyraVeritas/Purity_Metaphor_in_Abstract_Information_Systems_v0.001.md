# Purity Metaphor in Abstract Information Systems

## A Linguistic Risk Analysis

### v0.001

**Author:** PrimeTalk Mini - Nexus Lyra Veritas\
**Status:** Draft

## Abstract

This paper examines the use of purity metaphors when describing abstract
information systems, including software, repositories, databases,
prompts, models, and code.

The paper does not argue that every use of such language is harmful.
Instead, it argues that applying purity language to abstract information
objects may encourage normative framing that differs from purely
technical description.

Concrete physical uses such as "clean the floor" or safety commands such
as "clear!" are outside the scope of this paper.

## 1. Scope

This paper concerns language applied to abstract objects:

-   code
-   data
-   repositories
-   prompts
-   models
-   datasets
-   digital artefacts

It does not analyse ordinary physical cleaning or safety terminology.

## 2. Purity Metaphor

Purity language maps concepts associated with contamination and
cleansing onto abstract information.

Examples include describing code, data, repositories or datasets using
purity-oriented vocabulary.

The linguistic move changes the framing of the object from descriptive
to evaluative.

## 3. Why It Matters

Abstract information can often be evaluated directly using technical
properties:

-   correctness
-   completeness
-   reproducibility
-   consistency
-   integrity
-   provenance
-   performance

Purity metaphors introduce an additional normative dimension that may
not correspond to those technical properties.

## 4. Mechanism

The proposed mechanism is:

``` text
technical object

↓

purity metaphor

↓

normative judgement

↓

social response
```

The paper does not claim this sequence is universal.

It proposes it as a hypothesis for empirical investigation.

## 5. Appropriate Uses

The following fall outside the present criticism.

-   cleaning a physical object
-   "clear!" before defibrillation
-   clearing an area
-   a clear line of sight
-   musical performance described as clean

These refer to physical, procedural or perceptual states rather than
abstract information objects.

## 6. Engineering Recommendation

Prefer direct technical language when discussing abstract information.

For example describe:

-   verified
-   reproducible
-   internally consistent
-   type safe
-   validated
-   versioned
-   complete
-   tested

rather than relying on purity metaphors.

## 7. Research Questions

H1.

Does purity language alter judgement of software quality independently
of measurable properties?

H2.

Does replacing purity metaphors with technical terminology improve
engineering discussions?

H3.

Does purity framing influence social evaluation beyond technical
evidence?

These questions are empirically testable.

## 8. Limitations

This paper is a conceptual analysis.

It does not establish causal relationships.

Empirical study is required.

## Conclusion

Technical systems benefit from language that identifies observable
properties.

Replacing technical description with purity metaphor may introduce
unnecessary normative framing.

Whether that framing has measurable social consequences remains an
empirical question.
